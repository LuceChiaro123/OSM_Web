## Founding Story

Seven Florentine cloth merchants and noblemen — remembered as the **Seven
Holy Founders** — left their families and professions to live in poverty
and prayer on Monte Senario, near Florence. Only one is confirmed by name
in the earliest sources: **Bonfiglio (Bonfilius)**, the group's leader.
Tradition names the other six as Bonagiunta, Manetto, Amadio, Sostegno,
Uguccione, and Alessio.

Tradition holds that the founders saw visions of the Sorrowful Virgin,
pierced by seven swords — the origin of the Order's devotion to **Our Lady
of Sorrows**. The primary early source for this account is the *Legenda de
origine Ordinis fratrum Servorum Virginis Mariae*, likely written by Prior
General Pietro of Todi around 1317–1318.

The Order was formally and definitively approved by Pope Benedict XI's
bull *Dum levamus* on 11 February 1304, after earlier setbacks — the
Second Council of Lyons in 1274 had briefly threatened suppression of
newer religious orders.

## History Timeline

- **1240–1247** — the Bishop of Florence approves the group as a religious
  order
- **By 1250** — the first Servite priests are ordained
- **March 1256** — Pope Alexander IV confirms the Servites as an
  independent order
- **1260** — the Order splits into Tuscany and Umbria provinces
- **1304** — definitive papal approval, bull *Dum levamus*
- **1369** — the San Marcello al Corso convent is founded in Rome
- **1513** — Santa Maria in Via, a second Roman house, is established
- **18th century onward** — losses during the French Revolution and the
  Josephine suppressions in the Habsburg lands; the Bohemian province is
  lost entirely
- **1657 / 1709** — a Germanic reform province is formed and its
  constitutions approved
- **1907** — the reform and conventual branches' constitutions are merged
- **1987** — the Order's current Constitutions are approved
